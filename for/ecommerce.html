The problem

What gets in the way

The specific reasons this content usually stays in one language.

A catalogue is thousands of short videos

Per-video agency pricing works for a hero launch, not for a range where every SKU has its own thirty-second demo.

Sizes, units and claims change at the border

A “size 8”, a weight in pounds or a word like “waterproof” can be wrong, unclear or not permitted once the video plays on another country’s storefront.

Every marketplace has its own rules

Storefronts, marketplaces and social shops differ on length, format, captions and what may be said in a product video at all.

FAQ

Questions worth asking

Including the ones where the honest answer is a limitation.

Will it convert sizes, units and prices for us?

No, and you would not want it to guess. A translation renders what was said; whether “size 8” should become a different regional size, or pounds become kilograms, is a merchandising decision that depends on how you actually sell in that market. Put those decisions in the review brief and have the in-market reviewer correct the script before audio is generated. Prices are safest left out of the voiceover entirely.

What about product claims in other markets?

Claims are where a translated product video can create real exposure. “Hypoallergenic”, “organic”, “dermatologist tested” or a health benefit may be regulated differently in each market, and a faithful translation of a claim that is fine at home can be one you may not make abroad. Have each market’s claims checked by whoever approves your packaging and listings there, then lock the approved phrasings in the glossary.

Do marketplaces accept AI-voiced video?

Requirements vary by marketplace and change often — on length, format, captions, what may appear on screen and, increasingly, how synthetic media is labelled. Octavia produces the dubbed video and the caption or subtitle files; checking each marketplace’s current content policy before upload remains your job. Where a policy is unclear, a subtitled version of the original is the conservative choice.

Should we localize creator and UGC video too?

Carefully. A creator’s voice belongs to the creator, and most UGC and influencer licences cover reposting the video, not producing a version in which they appear to speak another language. Subtitle creator content by default, and voice it only where the creator has agreed in writing for those markets. Your own brand-produced video carries none of that complication, which makes it the better place to start.
